01 · Background03

Fracture morphology is spatial—not binary.

Cortical continuity

A plausible-looking surface can incorrectly close a clinically meaningful fracture gap.

Fragment displacement

Angulation, separation and articular depression depend on 3D relationships.

Safety consequence

High aggregate overlap is not evidence of reliable fracture preservation.

Project report §§1.1–1.2. The system is positioned as an experimental adjunct; it does not claim diagnostic or clinical readiness.

02 · Literature Review05

The literature compares systems—but rarely isolates causes.

Confound 01

Named networks bundle many choices

Topology, activation, normalization, capacity, loss and training commonly change together.

Confound 02

Datasets and geometry differ

Projection models, anatomy, preprocessing and splits can change apparent model ranking.

Blind spot 03

Aggregate metrics hide failure

Dice can stay plausible while small bones, surfaces or fracture gaps fail catastrophically.

So the research gap is not “Which network wins?” It is “Which decoder property causes the difference under controlled conditions?”

Better overlap did not guarantee reliable surfaces.

06 · Critical Analysis16

A plausible bottleneck: geometry-inconsistent lifting.

What generated the inputs

Finite-source DRR geometry

Divergent rays encoded source position, detector spacing and perspective.

╲│╱
What the model assumed

Cartesian axis replication

AP and flipped-LAT features were copied along orthogonal axes without a projection matrix or ray paths.

┼ ┼ ┼

Inference—not proven causality: uncommon fractures and small bones provide less evidence for learning this geometric correction from only 43 subjects.

Supported by persistent errors across frozen and joint-training regimes, but a matched Cartesian-vs-ray-lift ablation is still required.
07 · Impact & Responsibility17

Responsible value comes from limits made explicit.

Potential societal value

  • Additional 3D context where CT is delayed or unavailable.
  • Support for referral, education, planning and communication.
  • Lower-radiation inputs than CT for preliminary computational study.
  • Reusable controlled-ablation framework for small medical datasets.

Conditions before deployment

  • Expert-validated fracture annotations and clinical measurements.
  • External paired radiograph–CT testing across institutions.
  • Subgroup, privacy, safety and domain-shift monitoring.
  • Compute, energy, radiation, workflow and cost-effectiveness assessment.

08 · Conclusion18

What the controlled evidence supports.

01

Topology matters

Projected residual connectivity produced the strongest and most consistent improvement.

02

Activation was secondary

PReLU did not explain the apparent V-style advantage under controlled conditions.

03

Accuracy is not safety

Fractured cases, patella, fibula and empty predictions prevent clinical claims.

The contribution is a repeatable method for isolating architectural effects—and revealing where aggregate success still conceals failure.

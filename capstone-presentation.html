01 · Background03

Fracture morphology is a spatial problem and not binary.

Cortical continuity

A plausible-looking surface can incorrectly close a clinically meaningful fracture gap.

Fragment displacement

Angulation, separation and articular depression depend on 3D relationships.

Safety consequence

High aggregate overlap is not evidence of reliable fracture preservation.

Project report §§1.1–1.2. The system is positioned as an experimental adjunct; it does not claim diagnostic or clinical readiness.

02 · Literature Review05

The literature compares systems—but rarely isolates causes.

Confound 01

Named network architecture bundle many choices

Topology, activation, normalization, capacity, loss and training commonly change together.

Confound 02

Datasets and geometry differ

Projection models, anatomy, preprocessing and splits can change apparent model ranking.

Blind spot 03

Aggregate metrics hide failure

Dice can stay plausible while small bones, surfaces or fracture gaps fail catastrophically.

So the research gap is not “Which network architecture wins?” It is “Which decoder property causes the difference under controlled conditions?”
Report pp. 20–21, 38–51.

Potential risks identified during data exploration
Potential risk 01 · expanded evidence

Physical position—not folder names—defines the volume.

One study, two folders

VSD_010 is exported across separate DICOM series folders. Folder and filename order do not reliably describe where each slice belongs in the patient.

Safeguard: merge the series and sort slices using ImagePositionPatient along the physical slice axis.
If this risk is not controlled: reversed stacks, duplicated slices or gaps between folders can create an anatomically invalid volume that contaminates every later crop, target and DRR.
Potential risk 02 · expanded evidence

Laterality and target geometry must remain consistent.

Why this matters

The report found that absolute LPS X-position was sensitive to patient positioning and volume rotation. An orientation error can affect laterality, AP/LAT geometry, target overlap and physical-distance measurements at the same time.

Safeguard: canonicalize to LPS, then verify source-to-target chirality using the relative femur, tibia, patella and fibula relationships—not absolute X-position alone.

Add laterality evidence image here
Potential risk 03 · expanded evidence

Bilateral records must not leak or double-weight a subject.

Why this matters

VSD subjects frequently contribute both knees. Splitting those knees across folds can reward subject familiarity, while knee-level summaries can give bilateral subjects twice the influence of single-knee subjects.

Safeguard: assign folds by subject, audit that no subject crosses partitions, and aggregate bilateral observations before cohort summaries.

04 · Methodology15

The lift copies two feature planes into a 3D grid.

Shared 2D → 3D pathway
Begin with fused AP and lateral maps.

The lateral map is flipped to match the standardized LPS orientation.

Replicate each plane along an orthogonal axis.

The code permutes, inserts a new dimension and expands the feature map into a cube.

Concatenate both cubes and learn a 3D fusion.

A 3 × 3 × 3 convolution mixes the complementary AP and lateral evidence at every scale.

But the input rays were divergent.

The current lift assumes Cartesian replication and receives no source position, detector spacing or projection matrix.

Plausible bottleneck—not proven causality: a matched Cartesian-versus-ray-lift experiment is still required.

04 · Methodology16

The decoder grows coarse evidence into four bone predictions.

Matched 3D decoder skeleton
Start at 8³ with 512 channels.

The smallest feature volume carries the widest contextual view of the knee.

Upsample to 16³ and add the matching skip.

Transposed convolution doubles spatial resolution; concatenation restores scale-specific evidence.

Repeat at 32³ and 64³.

Each stage combines the growing volume with a finer lifted feature level.

Refine at 128³, then emit four logits.

One channel is produced for each bone before a shared trilinear upsample to 256³.

Convert logits into occupied voxels.

Sigmoid probabilities above 0.5 form the final femur, tibia, patella and fibula prediction.

The surfaces shown are saved model output. No fictional intermediate bone shapes are introduced.

04 · Methodology17

A residual path preserves the coarse guess while the block refines it.

The controlled architectural factor
Every arm receives the same input feature.

The encoder, fusion, lifting, decoder widths and training conditions remain fixed.

Both blocks apply two matched 3D convolutions.

Group normalization and the chosen ReLU or PReLU activation are applied in the same skeleton.

The residual arm adds a projection shortcut.

A 1 × 1 × 1 projection aligns channel width, allowing the original coarse signal to bypass refinement.

Learning becomes “refine this” instead of “rebuild everything.”

This is the topology change isolated by the 2 × 2 experiment on the next slide.

Important: the result concerns these controlled decoder blocks—not a universal claim that full V-Net always beats 3D U-Net.

03 · Critical Thinking20

Fold 4 lowered accuracy but does not affect the conclusion.

Why Fold 4 was weak

Representation quality fell

P2 pairing margin was 0.000079, only 5/9 subjects were positive, and feature retrieval and dimensionality were poorer. Every decoder therefore reached its lowest Dice.

What was done

The fold was retained

In the fold-level sensitivity analysis, removing Fold 4 changed the mean Dice gain only from about 0.044 to 0.043, so the decoder ordering remained robust.

Surface caution: a separate Fold-2 empty-patella case produced approximately 177 mm subject ASSD and was retained to avoid optimistic results.

03 · Critical Thinking25

Geometry-inconsistent lifting is the plausible system bottleneck.

Current lift

Projection evidence and lifted features disagree.

  • Finite-source DRRs contain divergent rays.
  • AP and LAT features are copied along Cartesian axes.
  • Fused voxels may therefore represent different physical points.
→
Matched future test

Change only the lifting geometry.

  • Use projection-matrix-aware ray lifting.
  • Or add a differentiable DRR reprojection loss.
  • Keep encoder, decoder, cohort, folds and training fixed.
This is a supported hypothesis—not a demonstrated causal result—because no matched lift ablation was run.

04 · Conclusion27

Residual topology matters more than activation choice.

Residual-ReLU was the strongest tested condition—but the project establishes a controlled evaluation method, not clinical readiness.

Best defensible claim: residual decoding uses the available 3D features more effectively, while absolute and fracture-specific performance remain limited.

Project contribution

A leakage-controlled factorial framework that isolates decoder topology and activation, then exposes bone-, surface- and cohort-specific failure—not only average overlap.

SDG 3.8 alignment

The work explores how widely available radiographs could eventually support more accessible 3D visualisation; this is research alignment, not demonstrated healthcare impact.
